LaTense: Measuring the Limits of Geometric Activation Steering

Nathan Egbuna
University of Pennsylvania     egbunanathan@gmail.com     Preprint, 2026

Claims that activation steering causes or prevents “latent collapse” are routinely supported by n-gram repetition rates. We show that this metric is governed by output length rather than by the intervention. We reach this through a controlled study of LaTense (Latent Sense), a dynamic steering framework that modulates intervention strength from the local geometric alignment between the hidden state and a reasoning vector.

By applying a cosine penalty $1 - \cos(h, v)$ and norm-proportional scaling $\frac{\|h\|}{\|v\|}$, LaTense is intended to act as a restorative force on inference trajectories. In practice the cosine gate is inert — measured alignment stays near 0.03, so a norm-scaling-only ablation reproduces the method's one significant gain exactly. We evaluate LaTense across three model families (Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Gemma 2 9B IT, and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) on StrategyQA, MATH-500, and TriviaQA. Our results characterize the geometric limits of activation steering: steering gains are strongly bounded by model and task. The clearest gain is on strategic reasoning with Gemma 2 9B IT, where LaTense reaches 74.20% on StrategyQA, +8.6 points over static CAA ($p < 0.0001$, paired bootstrap), at a single-pass cost of 122.7 generated tokens per problem. On the remaining model-task pairs LaTense performs at or below its baselines.

Motivation: The Latent Collapse Problem

Activation addition—adding a static steering vector $v$ across inference steps $(h' = h + \alpha v)$—has emerged as a compelling training-free method for controlling model behavior. However, static interventions apply constant, rigid pressure regardless of the model's instantaneous representational state.

Circuit Overwriting

Constant steering applies the same pressure to every token, including tokens where the model is already on a correct trajectory and factual recall is in progress.

Text-Looping Collapse

At high intensity, steering traps generation in degenerate loops. At $\alpha = 1.0$ we log 3-gram repetition of 80.9% and 94.4% on individual StrategyQA generations, and this affects LaTense as well as static steering. At the operating point ($\alpha = 0.3$) steering leaves repetition essentially untouched: 35.92% against 35.82% unsteered on Llama MATH-500, and 23.97% against 23.95% on Gemma. The collapse is a ceiling on usable intensity, not a failure mode any of these methods removes.

Inflexible Strength

A constant coefficient $\alpha$ over-steers states that are already well-aligned while under-correcting severely drifted tokens.

The LaTense Mechanism

LaTense dynamically modulates steering intensity per token through two geometric principles:

1. Alignment Attenuation (Cosine Penalty): We attenuate steering pressure proportionally to local directional alignment: $$G_{align}(h, v) = 1 - \cos(h, v) = 1 - \frac{h \cdot v}{\|h\| \|v\|}$$

2. Manifold-Consistency (Norm Scaling): We scale proportionally to the relative magnitude of the hidden state to prevent out-of-distribution norm displacement: $$G_{norm}(h, v) = \frac{\|h\|}{\|v\|}$$

Combining both geometric gates yields the closed-form LaTense intervention applied at each target layer: $$h' = h + \alpha \cdot (1 - \cos(h, v)) \cdot \frac{\|h\|}{\|v\|} \cdot v$$

A note on the cosine term. Across every logged run, the measured alignment between the hidden state and the reasoning vector is small, $\cos(h, v) \approx 0.02$ to $0.07$, so the penalty $(1 - \cos)$ stays near $0.97$ throughout generation. In practice the gate varies little, and the intervention behaves close to pure norm-proportional scaling. Consistent with this, the norm-scaling-only ablation matches full LaTense example-for-example on Gemma 2 9B IT StrategyQA.

Repetition, measured. The 0.00% claim is withdrawn, and the measurement that replaces it is more interesting than the claim was. Running unsteered greedy decoding over the same MATH-500 problems gives 35.82% (Llama) and 23.95% (Gemma) 3-gram repetition, against 35.92% and 23.97% under static steering: the intervention moves the rate by +0.10 and +0.02 points, and Qwen behaves the same way (39.13% steered vs 38.07% unsteered). Across 19,268 generations from 56 runs spanning six models from 0.5B to 9.2B parameters, repetition is predicted by output length (Spearman ρ = 0.881), not by whether or how the model was steered — TriviaQA sits at 0.00–2.03% on answers of 7–34 tokens, StrategyQA at 3.15–6.02%, and MATH-500 at 23.6–39.4% on derivations of 350–560 tokens. Genuine collapse appears only at intensities far above the operating point, where individual generations reach 80.9% and 94.4%.

What the metric is actually detecting. Shuffling the words within each generation — holding length and vocabulary fixed, destroying only word order — collapses repetition from 5.93% to 0.20% across 3,636 generations. So this is not a chance-collision effect of long sequences: it is genuine recurrence of surface form, the restated "divide, multiply, subtract, bring down" scaffolding of a long derivation. The metric therefore conflates task-intrinsic structural repetition with degenerate looping and scores both in the same 24–38% band, though only the second is a failure. A correct polynomial long division scores 68.9%. The fix is to report the excess over a shuffled control, or a length-matched unsteered reference, rather than a bare rate — otherwise the metric rewards any intervention that merely shortens its outputs.

It is not an artifact of choosing trigrams. Recomputing both the actual and the shuffled rate for $n \in \{2,3,4,5\}$ over the 3,446 retained generations of at least five words, the rank correlation with length holds at every order (ρ = 0.804, 0.769, 0.742, 0.693) and the excess over the shuffled control stays substantial (+8.32, +6.04, +3.96, +2.79 points).

It does not depend on model scale either. Across six models spanning a 18× parameter range, mean output length predicts mean repetition on MATH-500 (r = +0.967) while parameter count does not (r = −0.288). Within the Qwen family a 15× increase in parameters (0.5B → 7.6B) moves repetition by 2.5 points and not even monotonically (38.31%, 36.67%, 39.13%), whereas writing ~200 more tokens than Gemma moves it by 14. The high repetition once read as evidence of Qwen being "hyper-optimized" is reproduced by a 0.5B Qwen, and is explained by Qwen writing ~545-token derivations where Gemma writes ~355.

Efficiency: Single-Pass Steering

Sampling-based test-time compute methods such as Self-Consistency $(k=5)$ or Best-of-$N$ generate several full candidate chains and aggregate them, whereas LaTense runs on a single forward pass $(k=1)$ with small geometric gating overhead. On Gemma 2 9B IT StrategyQA, LaTense generates a verified 122.7 tokens per problem. Self-Consistency reaches 64.4% while generating five sampled chains averaging 113.2 tokens each, 565.9 tokens in total, giving the 4.6× reduction. Both figures are confirmed against the recovered run logs.
